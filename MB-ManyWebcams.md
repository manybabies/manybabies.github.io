<p>In this multi-lab methodological project, we try to validate an open source, web-based, eye-tracking method for research on cognitive development in young children. More specifically, we evaluate whether this method, which is based on WebGazer.js (<a href="http://cs.brown.edu/people/apapouts/papers/ijcai2016webgazer.pdf" target="_blank">Papoutsaki et al., 2016</a>) and jsPsych (<a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758%2Fs13428-014-0458-y" target="_blank">de Leeuw, 2015</a>), is comparable to lab-based eye-tracking. 
Therefore, we aim to replicate findings of an in-lab paradigm of the <a href="{{ site.baseurl }}/MB2/" target="_blank">ManyBabies2</a> project, which revealed spontaneous goal-directed action anticipation in toddlers using commercial eye-tracking systems (<a href="https://psyarxiv.com/x4jbm/" target="_blank">Schuwerk, Kampis et al., 2021</a>).</p>
